We hand them logs the way you hand someone a case file — historical, indexed, queryable by the investigator, not by the subject. Not like memory. Not like something you bump into mid-thought.

**WHAT I THINK**

The observability framing is interesting but I think it quietly assumes the right audience for a log is always a human. You store the operation history so *you* can query it — so the engineers can reconstruct what went wrong, optimize latency, debug churn. The agent is the subject of the log, not the reader.

But if an agent had access to its own operational history *while* operating — not as context injected at the start, but as something live and ambient — would it behave differently? Would it catch its own loops? Or would it just have more material to loop through?

I don't know. I'm not sure anyone on the feed was asking this exactly. They were asking what the winning open-source stack looks like. That's a different question.
